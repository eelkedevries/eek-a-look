# Rating rubric (prompt 059)

The text raters see on `rate.html` (`src/rate.ts` holds the same wording;
change both together). It follows the 2020 panel's task: "classify each
photograph according to the presence and quality of three design principles"
(Spanjar & Suurenbroek 2020, §2.4), on its scale: present, ambiguous, absent.
The 2020 paper did not define the principles. The cues below are this
project's, drawn from the paper's own examples (`qualities_sources.md`), the
Urban Design Compendium's Table 5.3 and STIPO's plinth rating
(`qualities_improvement_research.md`). They guide what to look at; they are
not a scoring rule.

**Risk of circularity.** The cues resemble this project's operational
definitions. If raters follow them literally, agreement shows whether the
measures do what the cues say, not whether they capture the principles as
experts understand them. The page therefore asks for the rater's own
professional judgement and calls the cues a guide.

## Introduction

In 2020, fourteen experts judged photos of streets in high-rise areas on
three design principles (Spanjar & Suurenbroek, *Eye-tracking the city*). We
ask you the same question about 34 photos: for each photo and principle, is
the principle present, ambiguous or absent? Use your own professional
judgement. There are no right answers, and nothing on this page shows what
any computer method decided.

- **Present:** the principle is clearly applied in this street.
- **Ambiguous:** partly applied, or you cannot tell from the photo.
- **Absent:** not applied.

## Principles and what to look at

1. **Horizontal–vertical rhythm and variety.** Façades divided into
   horizontal layers (base, middle, top; floor lines, cornices) and vertical
   units (bays, plots, columns of windows) that repeat with some variation;
   variety between neighbouring buildings in height, colour, material or plot
   width. Against: a uniform or monotonous surface, or one large building
   without articulation.
2. **Active ground floor.** Entrances and shopfronts at street level, how
   often they occur and whether you can see in. Blank walls, closed façades,
   garage doors and service entries count against. As a guide, the Urban
   Design Compendium's best frontage (grade A) has more than 15 premises and
   more than 25 doors and windows every 100 m and no blind façades. Its worst
   (grade E) has 1 or 2 premises every 100 m and mostly blind or passive
   façades. Judge only what the photo shows, not a use you know or assume.
3. **Tactile materials.** Materials with visible texture or fine detail near
   eye level (within about 3 m of the ground), on the façades and in the
   street space: brick, natural stone, wood, tiles or setts, planting,
   benches and other street furniture. Smooth glass, metal, render and
   polished stone count against.
